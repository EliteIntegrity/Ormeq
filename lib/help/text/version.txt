Ormeq 0.1.1

A fork of FrogComposband 7.1.salmiak, taken on 23 August 2026. Ormeq 
retains its inherited game systems in a setting shared with Ammerow: 
Lands Beyond. Its SDL3 presentation adds themes, optional portraits and 
tiles, creature cards, health warnings and The Reckoner's calculation 
explanations.

Official playable packages include sound effects and ambient 
soundscapes shared with Ammerow and Ormeq - Home Theme (Dark) by 
EliteIntegrity, which plays on the title screen. Effects, ambience and 
music have independent controls under Settings > Sound. The audio has 
separate asset terms; public source builds use the original synth and 
no music. See [a] for credits and the notices in the game folder for 
the complete terms.

The second number the V command shows you, 7.1.howe, is the savefile 
compatibility version. It is inherited and it is not a release of 
anything.


Where this game came from

Nothing here started here. In order:

Moria (1985, Robert Alan Koeneke), Umoria (1989, James E. Wilson), 
Angband (1990, Alex Cutler and Andy Astrand, then Ben Harrison and many 
others), ZAngband (Topi Ylinen, Robert Ruehlmann and the ZAngband 
DevTeam), Hengband, Chengband (Chris Kousky, Dave Zhang, Andrew Levy), 
PosChengband (Chris Kousky), ComPosband (Gwilim Owen), FrogComposband 
(Antero Sulka).

Ormeq is the newest layer on that stack. Its setting and presentation 
build on the game systems written by the developers above.

The full list is in credits.txt, beside the game, and LICENSE there too 
for the terms they pass down -- which include, and have included since 
1990, that this may not be sold.


Why there is no changelog here

This file used to hold about six thousand lines: the complete release 
history of ZAngband, Hengband and FrogComposband, twenty-five years of 
it.

It was removed on 11 September 2026, for two reasons.

It was never Ormeq's release history. A fork nineteen days old shipping 
somebody else's quarter-century of release notes, as though they were 
its own document, is at best confusing.

And it had been edited. This project runs renaming passes over its data 
files, and those passes were allowed to run through the changelog -- so 
a first-person passage signed by one of those authors ended up 
describing his own game in words he never wrote. That is not a small 
thing. A record you have edited is not a record, and the right repair 
was to stop keeping somebody else's.

The originals are intact where they have always been, with the projects 
that wrote them.
