The Reckoner: understanding the numbers

The Reckoner explains calculations the game has already made. It does 
not predict the next attack, roll extra dice, change your chances or 
spend a turn. Past Reckonings is its history. The live panel is 
optional; the history works with the panel off.

Controls

Press Ctrl+P and Tab to Past Reckonings. Click The Reckoner's Details 
button to open its displayed result directly. Each recent entry has a 
fixed #number that stays with it when you scroll. Click an entry to 
read it; Enter opens the selected entry. On small screens, L switches 
between the list and account. Wide screens show both together. Use n/p 
for older/newer entries and ]/[ for results within the selected entry. 
Up/Down or the mouse wheel scroll the account; Page Up/Page Down scroll 
a page. In the compact list, arrows select entries instead.

Press e for the full exact working, r to replay the diagram, and ? for 
this guide. In exact working, | exports text or HTML to lib/user, asks 
before replacing a file, and shows its path. Back, Export and Help are 
also clickable. Escape returns from an account to its list on small 
screens, then closes history. Results from the same player turn share 
an entry; a busy turn continues in another entry when a block fills. 
Ctrl+P still opens the history with the live panel off. Labels such as 
Casting, Damage, Healing and Status change identify what each result 
means.

In this guide and exact working, use Page Up/Page Down (or 9/3) to 
scroll. Up/Down moves one line. In the guide, > jumps to the next 
section and < to the previous one. Escape returns to the history.

Escape > Settings > The Reckoner cycles Off, Static and Animated. 
Animation follows recorded values; it never rerolls them or holds up 
play. Menus and full Look cards hide the live panel temporarily. Small 
windows use a compact, readable result card automatically; Details 
opens the full account. The panel and its camera clearance stay fixed 
as results arrive. Visible map beside the panel remains clickable.

Start with the result

Read the result's unit first. Damage dealt is an outgoing damage 
amount; it can exceed the target's remaining HP. Damage received is 
incoming damage, also labelled HP lost in the detailed calculation. HP 
restored is healing after the maximum-HP limit. Power is an input to an 
effect such as sleep, charm or teleportation. Power 60 does not mean 60 
damage, a 60% chance, or 60 turns.

One account may contain a casting check, several projectiles, different 
targets, healing and secondary effects. The live panel shows one result 
at a time. When several results share the latest recorded turn, its 
header shows a position such as 3/5. Click < or > to browse those 
results in place, including results from separate accounts in that 
turn. Click Details to open the result you are viewing. The live arrows 
and Details are mouse controls; they do not replace movement, running 
or stair keys. A new calculation selects the latest account again. The 
panel stays still; it does not cycle automatically or delay your next 
command. A + after the count means some results exceeded the recording 
limit. Older turns remain in Past Reckonings. A ball can reuse one 
damage roll for several targets; identical dice in those records are 
intentional. A later projectile may roll afresh.

The spell diagram reads from left to right: Dice / Base, then Cast 
(bonuses and spell power), At target (after distance), then Damage 
(after target defences). A fixed base means that stage did not roll 
damage dice. It may be calculated from level, health or the selected 
power level.

The large result and its short explanation come first. The explanation 
highlights the largest recorded change, or explains a status counter or 
check. Immediately below, the source, weapon or spell, and recipient 
sit together with the numerical stages, ribbon and dice. Glyphs 
preserve their appearance at the time; an unknown source stays a 
question mark. A star denotes magic or a generic effect.

The ribbon's thickness follows the amount at each stage, relative to 
the largest amount on that card. It is not remaining monster health and 
cannot be compared directly between different attacks. Signed changes 
under the stages are differences between those displayed amounts; exact 
working preserves every recorded intermediate step. Incoming damage and 
healing use fewer stages for short chains; longer chains include the 
first change and the value before the final change. Partial records 
remain labelled: a visible before/after value is not a claim that its 
formula is known.

Sparks travel along this already settled ribbon in Animated mode; a 
reduction sheds sparks. Their number is decorative, not one particle 
per damage point. Static mode keeps the same numbers and diagram. Dice 
faces and the range marker are actual recorded values. The centre tick 
marks the dice average, not a probability or expected final damage.

A worked damage example

These are illustrative numbers, not a promise about a particular spell 
or creature:


  4d6 means four six-sided dice.
  Actual faces:                 2 + 3 + 1 + 6 = 12
  Spell damage bonus:                  12 + 7 = 19
  Spell-power modifier +3:       19 + 19*3/13 = 23
  One square from the centre:     (23 + 1)/2 = 12
  Target takes one third:               12/3 =  4
  Resolved outgoing damage:                     4


The engine uses integer arithmetic and the order matters. In this 
example 19*3/13 becomes 4, so the cast value is 23. It is not rounded 
to the nearest integer. Integer division truncates towards zero; for 
positive values this discards the fraction. Some spells use different 
distance rules or full damage throughout their area. Exact working 
shows the rule actually used.

The dice average describes the dice before modifiers. It is not 
expected final damage. The position on a dice range is not a 
percentile: middle totals are usually more likely than extreme totals.

Shots and thrown weapons

Bows, slings, crossbows and thrown weapons record the actual hit check, 
damage dice and critical check. Their chains include observed slays or 
brands, flat bonuses, launcher or throw multipliers, ambush, 
concentration, stun and target defences where applicable. A bow shot 
adds its ammunition bonus before its critical and launcher multiplier; 
a thrown weapon adds its weapon bonus after the critical. Exact working 
preserves that order.

For example, a shot could roll 2d6 = 7, add an ammo bonus of 2 to make 
9, receive a critical multiplier of 150/100 to make 13, then a launcher 
multiplier of 250/100 to make 32, and add 4 launcher damage to resolve 
36. Both divisions truncate; multiplying everything together first 
gives the wrong answer. These are sample values, not a guaranteed 
weapon configuration.

The ordinary ranged hit check first draws 0..99: 0..4 hits 
automatically and 5..9 misses automatically. Otherwise, after any 
character-trait check, an attack draw in 0..(effective attack power - 
1) must reach armour x 3/4. Effective power already includes range and 
applicable bonuses or penalties. Individual armour thresholds and their 
hit-check inputs are always concealed: recall shows race armour, which 
does not establish the live creature's modifiers. This uniform rule 
also avoids revealing a hidden modifier just because a trace 
disappears. Special guaranteed or painted-target hits use their own 
rules.

Explosive and elemental ammunition records its supplied effect power 
separately from the damage to each affected creature. That power is not 
another damage hit. Targets reuse the original projectile dice. Each 
piercing or repeated hit has its own record when the engine makes a new 
calculation.

Some special cases remain partial: fatal needle tests conceal private 
monster HP; wounded-target crossbow bonuses and custom throwing 
callbacks can have boundaries without formulas. After-hit 
saves/statuses and ammunition breakage, return and catch checks are not 
comprehensively traced. A shot into empty space need not create a 
creature-damage record. These omissions do not affect the shot or throw 
itself.

Incoming hit checks and prevention

Incoming attacks can now have separate Defence results: Attack missed, 
Hit allowed, Attack repelled, Attack reflected, Attack dodged, Spell 
turned back, or an early substitution escape. These are decisions, not 
damage amounts. Hit allowed only passes the accuracy check; protection, 
damage reduction and secondary effects follow afterwards. Use the live 
arrows or history to follow the results from that turn.

Monster melee uses two stages. Its first draw is 0..99: 0..4 hits 
automatically and 5..9 misses automatically. Otherwise a draw in 
1..attack power must be strictly greater than armour x 3/4. A tie 
misses. This differs from the player's ordinary ranged check. Attack 
power includes the monster's effective level, blow power and any stun 
penalty. Drunken boxing can increase armour for this check without 
increasing its damage reduction.

For example, with a gate draw of 42, attack power 80 and armour 60, the 
armour threshold is 60 x 3/4 = 45. An attack draw of 45 misses; 46 
allows the hit. A later invulnerability check might still block its 
damage. These are sample values, not an extra roll or a prediction.

Protection from evil records the opposed save and the additional 
repulsion draw when reached. Reflection, nimble dodge and spell turning 
record their own comparisons. Foresight and psionic foresight record 
their actual prevention rolls; invulnerability records whether the hit 
penetrated. Shadow absorption and force bypasses have notes where 
applicable. Ethereal-body and stance reductions retain their 
minimum-damage draws. A failed early check does not mean that all later 
defences failed.

The records preserve short-circuiting: if an early check prevents an 
attack, later rolls are absent because the game never made them. A 
successful early escape is recorded separately from the later, 
after-hit substitution behaviour. Escape destination selection, 
pre-emptive counterattacks, mount interception, absorption/evasion 
modifiers before the main damage record and every unusual secondary 
effect are not yet completely explained.

Monster-dependent checks require known creature details and equipment. 
Unseen, disguised or insufficiently studied attackers keep their inputs 
concealed, as do private individual scaling values. An unseen source 
stays unidentified. Numeric secondary details are withheld 
conservatively when the source level or true form is unknown. 
Reflection checks are omitted while equipment is unknown: even naming 
an unsuccessful reflection attempt could reveal an unidentified 
property. A visible bounce still has its normal message. Learning 
something afterwards does not reveal a previously concealed record.

Casting checks and saving throws

A casting check decides whether you cast successfully. For example, a 
draw of 27 in 0..99 passes a failure threshold of 20: 27 is not below 
20. The spell may then meet a separate target saving throw. Successful 
casting does not guarantee damage or a status effect.

Different effects have different saves. Read the printed comparison, 
not a presumed universal rule. Monster saves being true means the 
monster made that save. A generic Target condition being true means 
that comparison holds; the following outcome or status change explains 
its effect. A higher number does not always favour you.

For example, a player-power draw of 18 in 1..50 and a monster draw of 
31 in 1..60 can be compared as 18 <= 31. That condition is true, so 
this particular save succeeds. Other saves compare one draw with a 
fixed threshold, give a unique creature another attempt, or skip the 
draw entirely because of immunity. Only checks actually reached are 
recorded; short-circuited checks do not secretly roll.

Status effects and healing

Damage and statuses are separate. Zero damage can still accompany 
confusion, sleep or a successful teleport. Checks and effects are 
listed in resolution order in exact working, including observed random 
draws, accepted status changes and available outcome notes.

Your own status counters can be recorded numerically. A player counter 
increasing from 6 to 12 is a gain of 6 counter units, not 12 HP or 
necessarily 12 turns. Recovery depends on the effect, speed and later 
actions. Monster timers are private: their records say applied, 
increased, reduced or cleared, without revealing the old or new 
counter. Stacking arithmetic that would expose those counters is also 
concealed.

Healing also has limits. If a spell offers 30 healing when only 8 HP 
are missing, the restored result is 8. Draining attacks can report both 
damage and a separate healing event. Delayed effects such as Ego Whip 
record their later saves and damage when they occur.

Why some information is missing

Concealed: The Reckoner respects what your character knows. An 
unidentified creature, a disguised creature or incompletely known 
equipment can hide detailed inputs. The knowledge snapshot belongs to 
the original event: identifying something later does not reveal the old 
hidden numbers. Concealed values are discarded, not merely hidden in 
the live panel. Monster-level saving throws require the level knowledge 
shown by recall. Individual HP and timers remain private even with full 
race lore. Fatal strikes, capture and HP-based special damage therefore 
give an outcome without an HP-derived amount.

Partial: A boundary records a value before and after an adjustment 
whose full formula is not available in that trace. It is not a 
fabricated explanation. A complete damage chain means that the recorded 
arithmetic joins from its explicit source to that damage result; it 
does not certify that every secondary effect or every game mechanic is 
represented.

No creature damage recorded: A projectile may hit empty ground, affect 
terrain, heal, or apply a utility effect. Its supplied power is not 
proof that it damaged a creature. Detection, summons, item changes, 
travel and other non-numeric outcomes may still rely on normal game 
messages. Some special attacks and device effects can still have 
boundaries rather than complete source arithmetic.

Omitted: The history keeps 48 accounts in this session, up to 24 
results per account, 20 arithmetic steps per result, 32 secondary 
details per result and 32 individual damage-die faces. Larger records 
explicitly report omissions. The bounded spell-provenance pool can also 
expire an unusually long-lived source trace; its actual value is 
retained but the record is marked partial, never attached to another 
spell. Earlier accounts roll out of history. Starting or loading a game 
clears history; it is not stored in the character save.

Unchanged: Arithmetic stages that leave a value unchanged are usually 
omitted. No dice shown can simply mean a fixed value. A zero or failed 
result is a real result, not missing data.

Checking an explanation

You do not need to audit the mathematics to play. Automated tests 
compare seeded encounters with recording disabled/enabled and all 
display modes. Additional development checks compare against the 
previous engine's outcomes and random state. The tests also recalculate 
recorded arithmetic independently, check roll ranges and comparisons, 
exercise immunity and stacking, and test hidden data and record limits.

If something looks wrong, export the exact working and describe what 
happened. The useful question is whether the record explains the 
observed result with the correct units and order. A plausible-looking 
number alone is not proof. The test suite is targeted evidence, not a 
guarantee that every possible combination has been exercised.
