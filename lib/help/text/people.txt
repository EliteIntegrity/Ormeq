Credits

Ormeq builds on the work of the developers, contributors and players 
credited below, in the order they arrived.

The Beginning

1980-1983 -- Robert Alan Koeneke writes Moria on a VAX at the 
University of Oklahoma, with Jimmey Wayne Todd. It is the reason for 
all of this.

1987-1989 -- James E. Wilson rewrites it in portable C as Umoria, which 
is how it escaped the VAX and reached everybody else.

The Scottish Game

1990 -- Alex Cutler and Andy Astrand build the thing we do not name, at 
university, on top of Umoria. Sean Marsh, Geoff Hill and Charles Teague 
carry it to version 2.0.

1993-1994 -- Charles Swiger maintains it through 2.5 and 2.6.

1995-2000 -- Ben Harrison takes it over at 2.7 and rewrites most of it, 
including the terminal layer that let it be ported to anything with a 
screen. Much of the code in this game is still recognisably his.

1997-2007 -- ports and frontends by Skirmantas Kligys, Robert 
Ruehlmann, Gregory Velichansky, Eric Stevens, Antony Sidwell and 
Sheldon Simms.

The Variants

1994-1998 -- the Japanese version, by Sitoshin, sayu, FIRST and TeO.

1998-2000 -- ZAngband. Old code by Topi Ylinen, new code by Robert 
Ruehlmann, and then the ZAngband DevTeam. Its Japanese version is 
Mitsuhiro Itakura's.

2001-2010 -- Hengband, by Mr.Hoge and the Hengband Development Team: 
Habu, Henkma, iks, Kuwa, Mogami and nsk.

2000s -- Posband, by Alexander Ulyanov and Graham Philips. A great many 
of its ideas are still here.

2011-2013 -- Chengband, by Chris Kousky, Dave Zhang and Andrew Levy.

2013-2018 -- PosChengband, by Chris Kousky.

2018 -- ComPosband, by Gwilim Owen.

2018-2024 -- FrogComposband, by Antero Sulka. This game is a fork of 
that one, and the great majority of what you are playing is his work 
and the work of everyone above him.

Ormeq

2026 -- EliteIntegrity: Ormeq's setting, creative direction and 
presentation, with AI-assisted development. Ormeq shares its world with 
Ammerow: Lands Beyond.

Sound Effects and Music

The optional sound effects and ambient soundscapes are shared with 
Ammerow. They were created under human direction with Claude 
Code-assisted Python synthesis: mathematical models and recorded seeds, 
without recordings, third-party samples or a generative-audio service.

Title music: Ormeq - Home Theme (Dark), directed, reviewed and approved 
by EliteIntegrity. Created with Claude Compose: AI-assisted code placed 
the notes, and licensed virtual instruments performed them in Ableton 
Live. Audio analysis informed revisions; no generative audio or music 
model was used.

The theme plays on the title screen. Effects, ambience and music have 
separate controls under Settings > Sound. Footsteps and menu feedback 
can be switched off individually. Background sound follows surface 
day/night or dungeon depth, lowers in menus and pauses when unfocused.

Audio is copyright (c) 2026 John Horton, to the extent copyright and 
related rights subsist. Normal play, personal backups, private 
modifications and gameplay coverage, including monetised videos and 
streams, are permitted. Redistribution or reuse of the audio requires 
written permission except where applicable law permits otherwise. 
Public source forks omit the audio; effects fall back to the synth and 
missing music stays silent.
