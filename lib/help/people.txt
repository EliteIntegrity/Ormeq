<style:title>Credits</style>

Ormeq builds on the work of the developers, contributors and players credited below, in the order they arrived.

<topic:Beginning><style:heading>The Beginning</style>

<color:U>1980-1983</color> -- <color:keyword>Robert Alan Koeneke</color> writes Moria on a VAX at the University of Oklahoma, with <color:keyword>Jimmey Wayne Todd</color>. It is the reason for all of this.

<color:U>1987-1989</color> -- <color:keyword>James E. Wilson</color> rewrites it in portable C as Umoria, which is how it escaped the VAX and reached everybody else.

<topic:Scottish><style:heading>The Scottish Game</style>

<color:U>1990</color> -- <color:keyword>Alex Cutler</color> and <color:keyword>Andy Astrand</color> build the thing we do not name, at university, on top of Umoria. <color:keyword>Sean Marsh</color>, <color:keyword>Geoff Hill</color> and <color:keyword>Charles Teague</color> carry it to version 2.0.

<color:U>1993-1994</color> -- <color:keyword>Charles Swiger</color> maintains it through 2.5 and 2.6.

<color:U>1995-2000</color> -- <color:keyword>Ben Harrison</color> takes it over at 2.7 and rewrites most of it, including the terminal layer that let it be ported to anything with a screen. Much of the code in this game is still recognisably his.

<color:U>1997-2007</color> -- ports and frontends by <color:keyword>Skirmantas Kligys</color>, <color:keyword>Robert Ruehlmann</color>, <color:keyword>Gregory Velichansky</color>, <color:keyword>Eric Stevens</color>, <color:keyword>Antony Sidwell</color> and <color:keyword>Sheldon Simms</color>.

<topic:Ormeq><style:heading>Ormeq</style>

<color:U>2026</color> -- <color:keyword>EliteIntegrity</color>: Ormeq's setting, creative direction and presentation, with AI-assisted development. Ormeq shares its world with Ammerow: Lands Beyond.

<topic:Audio><style:heading>Sound Effects and Music</style>

The optional sound effects and ambient soundscapes are shared with Ammerow. They were created under human direction with Claude Code-assisted Python synthesis: mathematical models and recorded seeds, without recordings, third-party samples or a generative-audio service.

Title music: <color:keyword>Ormeq - Home Theme (Dark)</color>, directed, reviewed and approved by <color:keyword>EliteIntegrity</color>. Created with Claude Compose: AI-assisted code placed the notes, and licensed virtual instruments performed them in Ableton Live. Audio analysis informed revisions; no generative audio or music model was used.

The theme plays on the title screen. Effects, ambience and music have separate controls under Settings > Sound. Footsteps and menu feedback can be switched off individually. Background sound follows surface day/night or dungeon depth, lowers in menus and pauses when unfocused.

Audio is copyright (c) 2026 John Horton, to the extent copyright and related rights subsist. Normal play, personal backups, private modifications and gameplay coverage, including monetised videos and streams, are permitted. Redistribution or reuse of the audio requires written permission except where applicable law permits otherwise. Public source forks omit the audio; effects fall back to the synth and missing music stays silent.
