<style:title>Quest Journal</style>

Press <color:keyword>Ctrl+Q</color> to open the journal. It is also available
from the knowledge menu. Reading cards and browsing the journal costs no
turns. It does not change quest progress, roll rewards or identify items.

<topic:Lists><style:heading>Active, Completed and Failed</style>

<color:keyword>Active</color> contains accepted quests, including those whose
objectives are complete but whose rewards still await collection. Those
entries are labelled <color:G>Ready to return</color> and appear first.

<color:keyword>Completed</color> contains finished quests. A quest closed
without a recorded completion is labelled Closed. Failed quests have their
own tab when there are any. Unaccepted and concealed quests are not listed.

On a wide screen, select a quest on the left to read its details on the
right. On a small screen, select a row and press Enter (or tap it) to open
its details. Escape returns to the list; from the list it closes the journal.

Use Up/Down or P/N to select quests. Tab changes lists; A, C and F select
Active, Completed and Failed directly. On a wide screen, [ and ] scroll
the details and PgUp/PgDn scroll a page. On a small details page, Up/Down
also scroll. X closes the journal from either view. The labelled controls
and quest rows can be clicked or tapped. ? opens this help.
When details extend beyond the page, clickable scroll controls remain at
the bottom of the screen.

The journal remembers the last tab, selected quest in each list and each
quest's detail scroll position, including after saving and loading.
Selection follows the quest when the list reorders. If the detail width changes,
the text rewraps and starts at the top; otherwise your place is retained.
If that quest leaves its list, the first entry is selected. If the Failed tab
no longer exists, the journal opens Active. Each character has their own memory.

<topic:Cards><style:heading>Quest conversations</style>

Requesting work opens an offer card with the questgiver, briefing, objective
and danger level. A accepts; Escape leaves the offer for later. Reading or
declining the offer does not accept the quest or roll its reward.

Completing an objective announces the next step in the message area without
opening a page over the fight. Ctrl+Q shows the complete record. Town quests
normally require you to return to the questgiver's building and request the
quest service again. The receipt shows their response and the item actually
awarded. Dungeon quests that award rewards immediately need no town turn-in.

All town questgivers have portraits in the playable artwork pack, including
the small-town contacts. Missing portraits use a glyph. Artwork is optional;
it does not affect quests and is not included in the public source download.
The next step, objective, directions and leave warning come before a small
questgiver panel on every screen. Records without a questgiver need no portrait
space. The full briefing remains available by scrolling; no text is removed.

<topic:Information><style:heading>What the journal knows</style>

The objective uses the existing quest rules. Named kill missions can show
their recorded count, for example <color:G>5/8 defeated</color>. Clear-site
quests do not list all remaining enemies: doing so would reveal creatures
you have not found. The briefing retains any information the questgiver
already supplies, but the journal does not add monster statistics, map
layouts, undiscovered entrances or unidentified artifact details.

Danger level is the existing quest difficulty indication, not a promise
that a character of that level can win. Read the warning before accepting:
many quests fail if you leave their site before completing the objective.
Accepting work does not itself put you inside that site.

Completed town quests retain a receipt from turn-in: the actual reward,
the questgiver's closing response and when you collected it. This record
survives saving and loading, using or losing the item, and later dialogue
updates. It is a historical description, not another copy of the item.
Reopening the journal never rolls, awards or identifies anything.

Existing saves remain supported. Quests turned in before receipts were
recorded say "Reward not recorded"; their old rewards cannot be recovered
from the quest record. A recorded "No item was awarded" is different: that
turn-in really did award no item.

Dungeon quests now save a separate reward record. Items are dropped on the
floor, not automatically put in your pack. The record describes only the
reward drops visible when the quest finished, with their knowledge at that
moment. It does not identify them, reveal unseen drops or track collection.
Blindness and hallucination prevent item recording. A later identification
does not rewrite the earlier observation. Known stat increases awarded at
completion are also recorded. Earlier saves cannot supply missing records.

For example, "2 Blue Potions" means two potions were observed being added to
the floor, not that you collected two or learned their effects. A merged stack
records the new amount only. "No floor rewards were visible" does not mean
no reward was generated: you may have been blind or unable to see its location.

Karrot and Troika assignments join Completed and Failed using their existing
patron histories. Each has a permanent assignment number, target, kill count,
location and date. The current assignment remains in Active. Their individual
rewards, punishments and closing speeches in earlier saves were not stored
and are not reconstructed. New assignments preserve the actual patron reply,
messages describing witnessed effects, visible reward drops and known failure
reason. Unseen drops and hidden item properties remain concealed. A transcript
records what you heard and experienced, not a list of secret mechanical effects.
Very long transcripts are capped and marked when further messages are omitted.
Troika gifts are recorded on the assignment that earned them; other successes
can correctly say "No new patron gift on this assignment". Current earned gifts
are also shown separately, so later rewards do not rewrite older assignments.
Every new assignment starts at the top of its details page.

Failed quests retain their known failure reason and the existing failure date
and character level. Leaving before completion and explicitly choosing to
abandon a repeatable quest are recorded separately. This adds no new way to
abandon a quest and changes no penalties or quest rules.
On compact screens the failure summary appears before the portrait.

A town quest's closing response is recorded only when you report back. Until
then the journal says "Not yet reported to the questgiver"; it never previews
their response. After reporting, the actual response and reporting date remain
available even if the dialogue is updated later. A recorded empty response says
"No closing response was given". Dungeon and patron failures have no town reply.

Old failures retain their existing date but say "Failure reason not recorded".
Earlier closing responses also say "not recorded". If an old failure still
awaits reporting, that new response can be saved, but its cause is not invented.

All towns' quest guidance summarises the
briefing, including alternate quests. They do not reveal map coordinates or
undiscovered entrances. Where a briefing gives no precise directions, the journal says
so. Recorded kill counts remain visible; clear-site quests do not count
unseen enemies.

Bulin's castle quests distinguish recovery from clearing a site. For
"I Hate Snakes!", picking up the lost whip completes the objective; killing
every snake is not required. The other castle commissions require defeating
every enemy at their site. Most pay through whatever you recover there and
give no separate item at turn-in. The Underground City has a separate reward.
The cards do not preview hidden loot or item properties. These are explanations
of the existing quest rules, not changes to them. Bulin uses a glyph when no
portrait is installed.

Hjolgar's Fighters' Hall commissions are dungeon hunts. The objective gives
the target and required count, and Location gives the dungeon and quest level
for your world, including replacement dungeons. Only eligible target kills on
the active quest floor count, not kills elsewhere. The displayed progress is
the game's actual quest count. Troll Slayer requires day-trolls. Complete the
hunt before leaving its floor, then return to the Hall for your trophy.

Paliro's Thieves' Guild commissions require clearing every enemy at the named
site. Items do not need to be destroyed. You may keep what you find; the guild's
payment is separate and requires returning after completing the objective.
The journal does not enumerate undiscovered enemies, disguises, secret doors
or hidden loot. Both guild leaders use glyphs when their portraits are absent.

Colmanwen's Manor commissions in Angwil distinguish clearing a site from
destroying one particular source of danger. The Concern and the book warehouse
require the latter; defeating every other enemy is not necessary. The journal
does not identify the hidden target for you. The other Manor commissions
require clearing every enemy from their site. Dragon Quest also distinguishes
the loot you keep from the separate reward collected at turn-in.

Alternate quests keep their own briefing and objective. In small-town modes,
these commissions can belong to Odwin, Uldrik or Kimuk instead. Directions
follow that mode too: the quarry is south of Angwil in the normal world, but
in the forest northwest of Outpost in the small-town version. No undiscovered
entrance is marked on the map. Colmanwen uses a glyph if no portrait is installed.

Town modes can assign work to different people. Return details follow the
current town mode. Quests without contact metadata retain a general return
instruction rather than inventing a person or a location.

Anambar, Thalos and Zul commissions have explicit objectives, directions and
closing replies, including alternate quests and the Angwilian Stables in
Instant mode. Some story requests are not inventory tasks: the crystal-drake
and mushroom commissions count kills, and the wreck commission requires site
clearance. Staff Recovery completes when you pick up the requested staff.
The objective explains the existing rule; it adds no new collection requirement.

Finished entries explain existing non-item outcomes: the Howes service
discounts, the Cop Quest home and Zul's restored teleport service. They do not
promise undisclosed future quests. Main-story entries explain the next step
without revealing a concealed quest. Killing Fields is retained for older
characters, but clearly marked as a legacy commission whose former entrance
and quest service are no longer offered. This does not restore that quest.
